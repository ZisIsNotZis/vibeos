The quiet craft of a clear brief

A good brief does not make the work smaller. It gives the work a shape.

Field notes · 20 August 2026

Start with the decision

Before the background, name the decision this document should help someone make. A reader can carry context lightly when the destination is visible.

Strong briefs are generous with constraints. They explain what matters, what is still unknown, and which trade-offs are acceptable.

Make the next step obvious

Every section should earn its place by moving the reader closer to action. Keep the most useful detail close to the moment it becomes relevant.

  • Put the outcome in one sentence.
  • Separate evidence from interpretation.
  • End with an owner and a next step.
Clarity is not a tone of voice. It is a kindness built into the structure.

A small practice

Read the brief once as a newcomer. Circle every phrase that asks the reader to guess. Then make the guess unnecessary.
